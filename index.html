How this forecast is built

  • Final turnout. Each county's expected ballots are its average turnout in the 2018 governor's, 2022 governor's and 2024 presidential races, measured against today's voter rolls. The 2026 vote already counted gets a small say (15%): a county whose early vote is running ahead of the rest of the state is nudged up, one running behind is nudged down, and the statewide total stays on the three-election blend. A county is never projected below the votes already counted.
  • Who they vote for. Each county starts from its own average two-party result in 2018, 2022 and 2024. One statewide shift moves every county together until the statewide result matches the target.
  • NPA voters. NPA voters have swung widely from one election to the next, so the fundamentals are adjusted for where they stand now. Polls that publish an independents' breakdown give the NPA split (recent polls count more, party-aligned sponsors count less, and the number is pulled a fifth of the way toward even because independents are a small slice of each poll). The page compares that with the NPA split the 2018/2022/2024 average implies on today's electorate (92% of Democrats and 88% of Republicans backing their nominee) and moves the fundamentals by the difference, weighted by the NPA share of the projected electorate. Republicans and Democrats who vote across party lines are built into those loyalty rates.
  • The target. A blend of the polling average and the NPA-adjusted fundamentals. Polls get more weight the closer Election Day gets, from about half at four months out to about 90% on Election Day. The polling average weights recent polls more (a poll loses half its weight every 14 days), larger samples slightly more, and party-aligned sponsors less. Undecided voters are split in proportion to the two candidates.
  • U.S. House. Each district starts from the 2024 presidential result on the new map the legislature passed in May 2026. The same statewide swing as the Governor and Senate forecasts (their average, compared with 2024) is applied to every district, so districts that are already lopsided move less. Districts with public polls (FL-08, FL-09) give those polls a quarter of the weight. There is no incumbency adjustment, and most districts have no polling at all, so the House ratings are lean-based estimates rather than poll-driven forecasts.
  • What it is not. A projection built from public history and polls, not a guarantee. The forecast, the polling average and the map are recalculated each time the page loads. Voter-roll sizes are a June 2026 snapshot.
